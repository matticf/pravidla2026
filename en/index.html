Where it is played

Rink

Floorball is played indoors on a rink of 40 × 20 metres. It is enclosed by a low plastic board with rounded corners. The ball can bounce off it and play goes on.

Principle

The game is played inside the board. The markings on the floor show who may go where and where play restarts.

Drawn to scale. Tap a number or an item in the list.

Youth

Smaller players, smaller rink

Youth competitions in Czechia are often played on a smaller rink. Its dimensions are set by the regulations of each competition.

The youngest age groups play only 3 + 1, meaning three field players and a goalkeeper, sometimes on a rink of just 18 × 10 metres, roughly half the dimensions. Two of them then fit side by side across a regular rink.

Who plays

Players, goalkeeper and referees

Each team has at most six players on the rink: usually five field players and one goalkeeper. The others wait in the substitution zone and rotate all the time.

Principle

A team plays with at most six players at once. Only the goalkeeper may catch the ball with the hands.

The team

  • Unlimited substitutions. At any time during play, even on the fly. The player leaving must cross the board first; only then may the new one enter the rink.
  • The goalkeeper can be pulled. A team may play with six field players and no goalkeeper at any time. Typically late in the game when it is losing.
  • Captain with an armband. Only the captain may talk to the referees.
  • Team staff. Coaches and other team members, five at most. They stand behind the players’ bench; only one of them may step in front of it, to give instructions to the players on the rink.
  • Calm on the bench. Players sit on the bench and nobody may put a foot on the board. The referees warn first; a repeat costs 2 minutes. The aim is not to punish but to make the atmosphere in the hall more pleasant.
  • Equipment. Field players wear shirts, shorts and knee socks of the same colour; only the goalkeeper wears long trousers. Jewellery is forbidden and taping it over is not enough. A bleeding player, or one with blood on the equipment, must stay out of play until it is sorted out.
Goalkeeper

Catches with the hands and has no stick

The goalkeeper kneels in the goal wearing a face mask and stops the ball with hands and body. A few extra rules apply:

  • Must stay in the goal crease. Having left it completely, the goalkeeper counts as a field player without a stick and may not catch the ball with the hands.
  • May hold the ball for at most 3 seconds.
  • May not throw or kick the ball through the air over the centre line.
  • May not pick up an intentional pass from a teammate.
  • May score directly with a throw; nobody else needs to touch the ball.
Referees

Two with equal authority

A match is led by two referees with whistles. They are assisted by the match secretariat, which keeps the time and penalties and writes the match record.

Stick

Light, with a plastic blade

It must be IFF-approved. The blade may be hooked by 30 mm at most. The ball is plastic with holes.

How long it is played

Game time

A match has three periods of 20 minutes. Time is effective: it stops at every whistle and starts again when play resumes. That is why a match lasts longer than an hour.

Principle

Only the time when the game is actually being played counts.

Time out

30 seconds to regroup

Each team may take one time out per match. The captain or the coach can request it.

Overtime

10 minutes, first goal decides

Only when a winner is needed. The first team to score wins and the game ends at once.

Shoot-out

Penalty shots after overtime

If overtime does not decide it either, five players from each team go one-on-one against the goalkeeper. If it is still level, they continue one shot each until one team leads.

Youth and lower-level competitions may play shorter periods, at least 2 × 15 minutes.

The most common offences

What’s not allowed

Floorball is a non-contact game. No pushing, no hitting the opponent’s stick and no high play. A lighter offence means a free-hit for the opponents, a more serious one a bench penalty.

Principle

You fight for the ball with the stick, not the body. The stick stays low, and neither the opponent nor the opponent’s stick may be pushed, hit or held.

Colour key

  • free-hitA lighter offence. The opponents get the ball and both teams stay at full strength.
  • 2 minutesMinor bench penalty. The player goes to the penalty bench and the team plays short-handed.
  • 2 + 2 minutesMajor bench penalty. Two 2-minute penalties in a row; an opponents’ goal cancels only one.
  • 2 + 10 minutesPersonal penalty. The team is short-handed for 2 minutes only; the offender sits longer.
  • red cardThe player is out for the rest of the match.
  • penalty shotAn offence spoiled a clear scoring chance; one player goes alone against the goalkeeper.

Inside the goal crease an attacker must try to avoid the goalkeeper. Contact the attacker made no effort to prevent is careless play.

Ordinary screening is fine. It is an offence when an attacker actively and repeatedly follows the goalkeeper’s movements only to obscure the view.

An extra penalty for whoever started the scuffle. At most one player can get it, even when nobody is penalised for the scuffle itself.

Hooking and reckless play

Hooking an opponent’s body with the stick, throwing the stick at the ball or recklessly knocking an opponent over.

And what is allowed

This is not an offence

  • Stopping the ball with the body, for example the thigh or chest (not the head or a hand).
  • Passing with the foot below knee level. A kicked goal does not count, though.
  • A shoulder-to-shoulder challenge for the ball.
  • Hitting the opponent’s stick after playing the ball cleanly first.
  • A high back swing when nobody is nearby.
  • Running outside the board. You must not play the ball from there, though.
How play resumes

After the whistle

When the referees stop play, it resumes with one of four fixed situations. Which one depends on why play was stopped.

Principle

Whoever commits the offence loses the ball. The referees do not blow the whistle when stopping play would hurt the team that was fouled.

Face-off

Two players face each other and contest the ball placed between their blades. Everyone else stands at least 3 m away. It starts every period and restarts play after a goal.

Hit-in

The ball went over the board. The team that did not touch it last takes the hit-in, 1.5 m from the board where the ball went out. The ball must be hit, not dragged.

Free-hit

After an offence. Taken where it happened, at least 3.5 m from the goalkeeper area. The defending team may form a wall. A free-hit may go straight into the goal.

Penalty shot

A defender’s offence spoiled a clear scoring chance. A player goes alone against the goalkeeper from an imaginary spot 5 m from the centre line and must keep moving towards the goal. Starting with the foot or a “zorro” move is not allowed.

Advantage

Why didn’t the referee blow the whistle?

When the fouled team still controls the ball and playing on suits it better, the referee lets play continue and just holds out an arm with the palm up. That is called advantage.

From mild to most severe

Penalties

For more serious offences a player goes to the penalty bench. Meanwhile the team plays short-handed, one player fewer. The opponents are on a power play.

Principle

The more serious the offence, the longer the team plays without the offender.

Delayed penalty

Arm up, play goes on

The fouled team has the ball, so the referee raises an arm and lets it attack. The penalty is given once the offenders gain the ball or play stops. Meanwhile the fouled team may pull its goalkeeper.

Two at a time at most

Always at least four on the rink

No more than two penalties per team are timed at once. Even with more players penalised, the team keeps at least four on the rink; the others wait on the bench for their turn.

Both teams at once

Penalties cancel out

If players of both teams are penalised during the same interruption, the 2-minute penalties on each side are added up. Whatever balances out does not change the number of players on the rink and is not cancelled by a goal. For example, 2 + 2 against 2 minutes means a power play for 2 minutes only.

New in 2026

A goal cancels the shortest penalty

When a team has several players on the penalty bench, an opponents’ goal ends the penalty with the least time left. It used to be the one awarded first.

When it counts

Goal or no goal?

A goal counts when the whole ball crosses the goal line from the front after being played correctly with the stick. It is confirmed with a face-off at the centre spot.

Principle

Goals are scored with the stick. An attacker cannot score intentionally with a foot or the body.

Goal counts

  • The whole ball is over the line, not just touching it.
  • The scorer played it with the stick. The goalkeeper can score too, with a throw, even if nobody else touches the ball.
  • The ball bounced into the goal off a defender, or a defender knocked it in (own goal). 702.1
  • An attacker unintentionally deflected it into the goal with the body. 702.2
  • A team playing without a goalkeeper spoils the opponents’ chance with an offence. Instead of a penalty shot, a goal is awarded straight away. 701.3

No goal

  • An attacker intentionally kicked the ball or directed it with the body. 703.2
  • The attacking team committed an offence just before.
  • The ball crossed the line after the whistle or the siren. 703.3
  • The ball entered the goal from behind or from the side.
  • The team that scored had a penalty being delayed against it, or had too many players on the rink.

Making sense of it

A short glossary

Words you will hear from commentators and from the stands.

That word is not in the glossary.

Stick
The implement with a long shaft and a hooked plastic blade used to play the ball.
Field player
Any player other than the goalkeeper, that is, one who plays with a stick.
Board
A low plastic wall with rounded corners around the rink.
Goal crease and goalkeeper area
Two rectangles in front of the goal: the larger goal crease is the goalkeeper’s territory, and field players must stay out of the smaller goalkeeper area.
Face-off
A contest between two players for the ball placed between their blades.
Hit-in
The restart after the ball has left the rink.
Free-hit
The restart after an offence, taken where it happened.
Penalty shot
One player going alone against the goalkeeper.
Short-handed and power play
Playing with fewer or, on the other side, more players while someone is serving a penalty.
Advantage
The referee lets play continue because stopping it would hurt the fouled team.
Effective time
The clock runs only while the game is actually being played.
Match record
The official document with the team lists, goals and penalties.
